Abdulwahab Khalifa Al-Ghanim

1881 to 1965

Life in brief

Nakhuda Abdulwahab bin Khalifa Al-Ghanim, eldest son of Khalifa Al-Shaheen and Sharifa bint Abdulwahab Al-Qatami, gave his life to the sea and kept commanding sailing ships until sail trading ended. He knew the kamal, the tables and the pilot books, and in 1939 he pressed Alan Villiers to teach him the sextant and chronometer, wishing Kuwait had a school of navigation. He trained his son Ghanim to command ships and died in 1965, aged about 84.

Alan Villiers
Alan Villiers met him in 1939 as «Abdul-wahhab Kalifa». He had sailed his big boom from the Rufiji two weeks ahead of Villiers and was at Mutrah when they arrived; «Old Abdul-wahhab Kalifa was always after me to instruct him in the mysteries of sextant, chronometer, and tables, and often expressed his regret that there was not a school of navigation in Kuwait.» At Manama the morning sun showed «the big boom of Abdul-wahhab Kalifa, also full of mangrove poles, coming in». Villiers also wrote that his father, «Kalifa al-Ganim, father of the nakhoda Abdul-wahhab», gave him an old shipmasters' directory of the Eastern Seas (Alan Villiers, Sons of Sindbad, 2006 edition, pages 263, 264, 269 and 281)
