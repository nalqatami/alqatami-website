Abdulwahab Eissa Al-Qatami

1898 to 1967

Life in brief

Nakhuda Abdulwahab bin Eissa Al-Qatami learned the sea from his father Eissa and became one of Kuwait's true navigators. He commanded the boom Ibn Rashdan and, with his brother Yousef, the great boom Samhan on long voyages to India, Aden and East Africa. In 1938 a British officer on HMS Shoreham certified that he had an excellent knowledge of navigation and pilotage. In 1948 he added a supplement on fishing, voyaging and sea trade to his father's Dalil Al-Muhtar.

Life facts

Born
1898, Sharq, Kuwait
Died
15 December 1967, Kuwait
Mother
Moza Shaheen Mohammed Ghanim Abdullah Al-Ghanim
Early voyages
At ten his father, Captain Eissa Al-Qatami, took him to sea on a baghla belonging to the Al-Abduljalil family to train him in navigation and command. After two years he sailed with his uncle, Nakhuda Abdulaziz Al-Qatami, then with his father on their new ship, Al-Kuwaitiya, for three years
First command
In 1915, aged 17, he became a nakhuda for the first time and brought his father's ship Al-Kuwaitiya from India to Kuwait. His father later bought him a boom, which he sailed to the ports of western India through the First World War
Boom Ibn Rashdan
The merchant Mohammed Thunayan Al-Ghanim chose him to captain the famous Boom Ibn Rashdan, which he sailed to India, Yemen and East Africa. It became Kuwait's leading ship in the 1930s. He handed it to his brother, Nakhuda Yousef, in 1938
Boom Ibn Rashdan
Bin Rushdan was built for Hamad Al-Saqr about 1920 and sold to Mohammed Thunayan Al-Ghanim and his brothers Thunayan and Shaheen; her first nakhuda was Abdulwahab bin Eissa Al-Qatami, and she was named Samhan or Ghalib; later she passed to his brother Yousef bin Eissa, and in 1943 she was sold for 14,000 rupees at Kung (Abu Ahmed, quoted on Instagram)
1936 rescue
Mohammed Thunayan Al-Ghanim chose him to lead a group of Kuwaiti nakhudas to rescue the Kuwaiti ship Boom Walad Mubarak and bring it home
1938
In the summer of 1938 he passed the navigation exam set by the British captain of the Shoreham, which was visiting Kuwait. He then gave up command and was general director of Kuwait Municipality, and after the Majlis events of 1938 he left Kuwait and lived in Basra for several years, Basra, Iraq
Trade
Came back to Kuwait in 1943 and traded between Kuwait and India until 1948
Trade
On 16 June 1943 Ahmed Al-Bishr Al-Roumi went out to the steamship with his brother Ali to meet Abdulwahab bin Eissa Al-Qatami on his arrival (diary, p. 98)
Writing
Added an appendix on diving and Kuwaiti sailing to his father's book, Dalil Al-Muhtar fi Ilm Al-Bihar
Interview
Recorded an interview for the programme «Pages from Kuwait's History» in 1965
Alan Villiers
In 1967 Captain Alan Villiers came to Kuwait a second time while he was on his sickbed, and wrote about it in the introduction to Sons of Sinbad
Sea
While working in Basra he supplied the boom Al-Yardila with dates for Nakhuda Ibrahim Al-Othman's voyage to India (Dr Abdulmohsen Abdullah Al-Kharafi, Al-Othman: Madrasat Al-Safar Al-Shira'i fil-Kuwait, page 154)
Sea
When the boom Walad Mubarak struck a reef off Manama, merchants and captains of Kuwait sailed to Bahrain on a jalboat, probably Al-Yazi, commanded by Nakhuda Abdulwahab bin Eissa Al-Qatami, and saw the ship repaired and brought safely home (Dr Yacoub Yousef Al-Hijji, Sina'at Al-Sufun Al-Shira'iya fil-Kuwait, page 201)
Sea
Earned a certificate from the British Royal Navy in 1938 (Khalid Salem Mohammed, Al-Musannafat Al-Milahiya, Al-Bayan, p. 65)
Khor Mian 1933
In 1933 a fight broke out at the port of Khor Mian in India between Kuwaiti sailors and men of the town. Among the Kuwaiti ships in port was the boom Ibn Rashdan under Nakhuda Abdulwahab Al-Qatami (Fouad Ibrahim Buarki, Al-Safar Al-Shira'i wal-Milaha fi Kuwait Al-Madi, page 336). The book does not give his father's name; Ibn Rashdan was his ship in the 1930s
Samhan
Al-Hijji writes that the boom Samhan, about 375 tons and perhaps the most beautiful deep sea boom ever built in Kuwait, was commanded by Abdulwahab and his brother Yousef. She was built in 1917 by Hajji Salman for Hamad Al-Saqr, and Muhammad bin Rashdan fell in love with her at her launch and bought her for 42,000 Indian rupees. In 1939 she outran a fleet of eight dhows from Matrah to Manama. About 1943 she was sold to a Persian merchant of Kung, and a few years later she foundered on a reef at the entrance to Lamu (Yacoub Yusuf Al-Hijji, The Art of Dhow-building in Kuwait, pages 124 and 125)
Navigator
Dr Yacoub Al-Hijji names him among Kuwait's true navigators: Nejdi himself told Villiers that Abd al-Wahhab al-Qitami was one, and his Certificate of Navigation, given on 2 July 1938 by a lieutenant of HMS Shoreham, said he had «an excellent knowledge of both arts». Al-Hijji writes that had Villiers sailed with Abd al-Wahhab al-Qitami and not with Nejdi, «a more positive picture of Kuwaiti sailing and navigation would have emerged» (Anthony R. Constable and William Facey (eds), The Principles of Arab Navigation, Arabian Publishing, 2013, pages 84, 89 and 90)
Navigator
Al-Roumi’s editor calls him one of the famous nakhudas, holding an English certificate, and the son of the late Eissa Al-Qatami, author of Dalil Al-Muhtar fi Ilm Al-Bihar (diary, p. 114, note 1)
Lived
Al-Roumi records him in Al-Zubair and Basra: lunch at his home in Al-Zubair in July 1946, and in July 1952 he met the Al-Roumi family at Basra airport and drove them to his house (diary, pp. 114, 115 and 177), Basra, Iraq
Works
Edited and reprinted his father’s Dalil Al-Muhtar fi Ilm Al-Bihar and added his own appendix, Al-Sayd wal-Tanaqqul wal-Tijara fil-Bihar (Al-Bayan, p. 65)
