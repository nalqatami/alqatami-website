Yousef Eissa Al-Qatami

1908 to 1981

Life in brief

Nakhuda Yousef bin Eissa Al-Qatami, born in 1908, was the son of the navigator Eissa. He commanded the great boom Samhan, which Alan Villiers called «a big beauty», on long voyages from Basra to India, Aden and East Africa, with young navigators training at his side, and he is counted as the first Kuwaiti to reach Port Sudan. He stopped sailing in the late 1940s, kept an office in Souq Al-Tujjar, and died in 1981.

Life facts

Born
1908, as given by his nephew Sager Abdulwahab Al-Qatami (Ma'alim Madinat Al-Kuwait Al-Qadima, part 7, page 43, quoting Al-Qabas)
Died
1981, aged 73 (Al-Hajji, Nawakhdhat Al-Safar Al-Shirai, p. 52), Kuwait
Died
1981 (his son Yacoub's biography)
Sea
Learned navigation from his Al-Shaheen Al-Ghanim relatives and commanded Boom Ibn Rashdan, one of Kuwait's best known ships, on voyages Alan Villiers recorded in Sons of Sinbad. The shipbuilder Ali Abdulrasool called him a fearless nakhuda. He is counted as the first Kuwaiti to reach Port Sudan on the Red Sea
Later life
Stopped sailing in the late 1940s and kept an office in Souq Al-Tujjar where he received nakhudas, merchants and sailors every morning
Boom Bayan
He owned the boom Bayan, of 3,000 mann, built by Ostad Ahmed bin Salman Al-Ostad (Fouad Ibrahim Buarki, Al-Safar Al-Shira'i wal-Milaha fi Kuwait Al-Madi, page 100)
Alan Villiers
Alan Villiers sailed home up the Gulf of Oman in company with his big boom Samhan, «commanded by the well-known Kuwait nakhoda Yusuf bin Isa». He described him as «a big, bluff, hearty man, six feet tall and built in proportion, who always laughed uproariously at his own jokes», who came visiting in a long white gown under a Japanese umbrella, and wrote: «I liked Yusuf bin Isa, and it was a pleasure to visit his big clean boom.» The Samhan had a little cabin in the poop with electric light and a radio, and her discipline was excellent. At Manama she was the first ship they saw, flying her Kuwait flag to welcome them (Alan Villiers, Sons of Sindbad, 2006 edition, pages 271 to 273, 279 to 281, 283, 289, 322 and 352)
Samhan
Al-Hijji writes that the boom Samhan, about 375 tons, was commanded by Yousef and his brother Abdulwahab. Built in 1917 by Hajji Salman for Hamad Al-Saqr, she was bought by Muhammad bin Rashdan for 42,000 Indian rupees, sailed to almost every major port of southern Arabia, western India, East Africa and the Red Sea, and was sold about 1943 to a Persian merchant of Kung (Yacoub Yusuf Al-Hijji, The Art of Dhow-building in Kuwait, pages 124 and 125)
Navigator
The editors of Sons of Sindbad: The Photographs write that Yusuf Al-Kitami took the Samhan on two long deep-sea voyages in 1938 and 1939, India to Aden and Mangalore to Mombasa, and that the two navigators aboard «were trainees, learning at his feet» (William Facey, Yacoub Al-Hijji and Grace Pundyk, Sons of Sindbad: The Photographs, Arabian Publishing, 2006, pages 18 and 19)
Source
Nawakhidhat Al-Safar Al-Shira'i fi Al-Kuwait, Dr Yacoub Yousef Al-Hajji, 2004, posted by alghanim_history, 10 November 2020
