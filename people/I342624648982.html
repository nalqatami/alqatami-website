Eissa Abdulwahab Al-Qatami

1870 to 1929

Life in brief

Eissa bin Abdulwahab Al-Qatami, remembered as the navigator of Kuwait, was born in Kuwait in 1287 AH (about 1870). He was a nakhuda and a man of letters, and his Dalil Al-Muhtar became the navigation guide many Gulf nakhudas kept in their sea chests. He sailed to India and East Africa, taught the sea to his sons Abdulwahab and Yousef, and in 1927 settled in Muscat, where he died in 1348 AH (1929) and is buried.

Life facts

Born
1287 AH (1870) (Al-Hajji, Nawakhdhat Al-Safar Al-Shirai, p. 42), Kuwait
Died
1348 AH (1929 or 1930); buried in Muscat (Al-Hajji, p. 46), Muscat
Biography
Al-Zirikli's biographical dictionary Al-A'lam (volume 5) gives his years as 1287 to 1348 AH, 1870 to 1929, and notes his navigation manuals
Early records
Papers of the Al-Saqr family from 1896 and 1897 record his voyages and trade with the sons of Abdullah Al-Saqr, including a list of the crew aboard his ship (Dr Faisal Al-Wazzan, Kuwait Center for Research and Studies, 2019)
His book abroad
Handwritten copies and adaptations of his navigation guide were used by captains in Hadramout, Yemen, one of them made by Captain Ahmad Awadh Eidid (Mohammed Alawi Baharoun, Hadramout Cultural Magazine, issue 10)
His book abroad
The navigators of Sur in Oman counted Dalil Al-Muhtar among their rahmaniyat, and an Omani navigator’s manuscript corrected parts of it (Mohammed Al-Ghailani, Naval History of Omani Sur, MA thesis, Yarmouk University, 2007, pp. 68 and 69), Sur, Oman
Sea
Fouad Buarki calls him Kuwait's first rubban (master navigator): born in Kuwait in 1287 AH, a sailor and man of letters who sailed to India and the African coast, and author of the navigation guide Dalil Al-Mukhtar. In 1897 he captained a shu'i of the merchant Abdullah Al-Saqer carrying Basra dates to Bombay, Karachi, Calicut and the ports of western India. His sons Abdulwahab and Yousef learned the sea from him (Fouad Ibrahim Buarki, Al-Safar Al-Shira'i wal-Milaha fi Kuwait Al-Madi, page 137)
Alan Villiers
On the voyage home Alan Villiers was shown «a well-thumbed copy of Isa Kitami's Arab directory of the eastern seas», his Dalil Al-Muhtar, and found from a quick look that it held good descriptions of landmarks (Alan Villiers, Sons of Sindbad, 2006 edition, page 254)
Navigation method
The Principles of Arab Navigation uses his own worked example from Dalil Al-Muhtar to show how a nakhuda found his ship's position after changing course: a four-stage voyage steered by Arcturus rising, Achernar setting, Capella rising and Ursa Minor setting, 93 miles in all, set out in a table and chart (Anthony R. Constable and William Facey (eds), The Principles of Arab Navigation, Arabian Publishing, 2013, pages 42 and 43, citing al-Qitami 1976, page 131)
Poetry
A zuhairi poem signed Eissa Al-Qatami, addressed to «Saqr», was printed on a magazine page of zuhairiyat; the family confirms it is his
Poetry
Al-Ba’tha printed his verses in five Arab dialects, Egyptian, Hijazi, Kuwaiti, Bahraini and Hasawi, and later more of his dialect verses on Al-Qibla and on grammar (1949, pp. 46 and 134)
Works
Ibrahim Al-Khalidi writes that Dalil Al-Muhtar fi Ilm Al-Bihar was printed outside Kuwait in 1942 and that ship owners carried it to find their way (Dakhalna Baladan Yuqal Laha Al-Kuwait, 2011, p. 210)
Works
He finished writing Dalil Al-Muhtar in 1334 AH (1915 to 1916), and it was first printed at Dar Al-Salam Press in Baghdad in 1342 AH (1923 to 1924); his house stood in Sharq (Jalal Al-Hanafi, Mu’jam Al-Alfaz Al-Kuwaitiya, 1964, pp. 197 and 416), Baghdad, Iraq
