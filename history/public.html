1950s onward

From the sea to public life

Jassim Abdulaziz Al-QatamiLolwa Abdulwahab Al-QatamiSharifa Abdulwahab Al-Qatami

As sail ended, the next generation moved into Kuwait's new state. British Foreign Office files show Jassim Abdulaziz Al-Qatami as Director of the Police Department in 1954 and 1955, resigning during the Suez crisis of 1956, serving as Under Secretary of Foreign Affairs until 1963 and being elected to the first National Assembly that year. The same files record Lolwa Al-Qatami leading women to register to vote in 1984 and 1986.

The Al Zayed book adds that Jassim was born in 1927 and was the first Kuwaiti officer to graduate from the police college in Egypt. He became Under Secretary of Foreign Affairs with the rank of ambassador on 27 March 1962, sat on the Constituent Assembly, was elected to the National Assembly in 1963 and resigned in 1965, and was elected again in 1975 and 1985. The book calls him one of Kuwait's national figures.

Abdulwahab bin Eissa made sure his daughters were educated: he sent them to the French mission school in Basra and then to Britain. His daughter Sharifa joined the Kuwait Oil Company in 1958; Al-Bayan (2024) calls her the first Kuwaiti woman to work at an oil company. Her sister Lolwa graduated from Edinburgh in 1960. Lolwa later told her own story in Memories of the Nukhatha's Daughter and her account of Kuwait in the 1960s in History and Achievements, both published in 2021, and in 22 episodes of the television series The Black Box.
