1930s to 1995

Abdulrahman, Abdulla and Mohammed, sons of Abdulaziz

Abdulrahman Abdulaziz Al-QatamiAbdulla Abdulaziz Al-QatamiMohammed Abdulaziz Al-Qatami

Abdulrahman went to sea only because his father insisted. On his first voyage the ship grounded off the Iranian coast and Baluch raiders came aboard; he let them load dates until the tide rose, then cut the anchor ropes and sailed away. He gave up the sea in 1937 for business in Kuwait, and told Al-Hijji in 1991: «The sea is sometimes like the Day of Judgment.» He died in 1995.

Abdulla trained under his cousin Abdulwahab bin Eissa on Bin Rushdan and commanded ships from the early 1930s to the late 1940s. In the 1938 and 1939 season he sailed to East Africa alongside his close friend Nakhuda Ali Al-Najdi, the voyage Villiers recorded in Sons of Sinbad. His ship sank after a collision with a steamer in 1940, and the crew survived. He died at home in April 1995.

Mohammed commanded the family boom Al-Hassan to Bharuch in 1936 and to Mangalore in 1938, until he was appointed to Kuwait's sea customs. His nephew Sager remembered him as the first head of Kuwait's coast guard, in 1938. He was known more for politics than for the sea, and in 1939, the Majlis year, he was killed in one of Kuwait's markets. His brother Abdulwahab, then on the Dau, had warned him: «Al-Qatami are people of the sea, not people of politics.» The Kuwaiti nakhudas in Muscat climbed aboard one by one to give Abdulla the news, and Abdulla said he wished Mohammed had never left the sea.
