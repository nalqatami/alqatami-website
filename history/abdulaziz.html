1906 to 1941

Abdulaziz bin Abdulwahab and the baghla Al-Badri

Abdulaziz Abdulwahab Abdulaziz Al-Qatami

Eissa's brother Abdulaziz was nakhuda of Al-Badri, the family's famous baghla, sailing every season to the ports of India's west coast. He belonged to the older generation of Kuwaiti nakhudas, with Ahmed bin Naser, Ahmed Al-Ghanim, Khalid Al-Asousi and Ali Al-Mubarak. In 1910 he was at Calicut loading timber, with his young son Abdulwahab learning beside him. In 1911 he unloaded dates at Khor Mian and sailed on to Calicut.

Sheikh Mubarak Al-Sabah sent him to Bombay to bring the ruler's new steam yacht Mushrif home to Kuwait, the first steamship bought in Kuwait. Al-Hijji dates this to 1906 and Al-Wazzan to 1907. From 1912 Abdulaziz stopped commanding ships himself. He travelled to India by steamer, bought rice, coffee, sugar and timber, and sent them home on Al-Badri, which his son Abdulwahab now commanded. The Al-Matrook trading ledgers of 1911 to 1914 record him and the Al-Badri in this trade.

He was so devout that he was nicknamed «Hamamat Masjid», the dove of the mosque, and he was respected in Kuwait and had friends in many Indian and Gulf ports. He married Ruqaya bint Abdulrahman bin Al-Sheikh Faraj and Aisha bint Ahmad Al-Oraifan, and died in 1360 AH (1941). The court heirs announcement names both wives and all his children.
