1917 to 1963

Abdulwahab bin Abdulaziz and the Dau

Abdulwahab Abdulaziz Al-Qatami

Abdulwahab bin Abdulaziz was a nakhuda before he was twenty and one of Kuwait's great mu'allims by thirty. His father handed him Al-Badri in 1917 and he sailed her to India every season until 1923. After two years commanding a ship for a merchant of Kharg, with his son Bader training beside him, he came back to Al-Badri around 1925. In 1936 he took command of Al-Saqr's famous boom, known as the Dau.

Alan Villiers met him at Zanzibar when he brought the Dau into port, and watched him bring the Dau into a crowded harbour before dawn, in the dark and in gusting wind, and anchor her beside the small boom of his half brother Abdulla without a single lamp. Villiers wrote that he did not know how it was done, and that at night it was close to a miracle. In 1938 and 1939, at Berbera, he held a Kuwaiti evening of music on the Dau's carpeted deck for the British consul, who gave him a letter of recommendation for the ports of Kenya, Tanganyika and Zanzibar. In the 1941 and 1942 season he showed Violet Dickson, wife of the British Political Agent, over the Dau.

He trained his sons and relatives himself. His sister's son Khalid Mohammed Al-Shaheen, who later became the dean of the Al-Ghanim family, first went to sea with him, and remembered dawn prayer on deck, then Quran, then letter writing, then charts, and at noon the sun sight for latitude and longitude. All his sons became known nakhudas, and a Swahili singer in Zanzibar learned sea songs from him. Later he ran his sons' voyages like a family company, bought the boom Fath Al-Khair, known as Al-Namsa, in 1946, the boom Al-Baz in 1949 and a motor launch from the Muscat government in 1950. When sail ended he traded in timber and building materials with his son Naser until he died in Kuwait City in 1963, the year both Al-Hajji and the Al Zayed book give.
