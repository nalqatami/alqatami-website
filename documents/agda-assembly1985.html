1985 · British Embassy telegram on the National Assembly

Jassim Al-Qatami on the Government walkout, 1985

Jassim Abdulaziz Al-Qatami

What it is

After losing two votes, the Government refused to take part in a third and most Ministers left the chamber. The Embassy reports that some deputies, including Jassim Al-Qatami, said this refusal hardly matched the Government's own calls for cooperation between the executive and the legislature.

Why it matters

Jassim back in the Assembly in 1985, still defending parliamentary procedure.

Who is meant

The same Jassim Al-Qatami as the records that give his full name: Director of Police, resigned at Suez, later a nationalist member of the National Assembly.

Page copies

Original text

3. The Government has said that despite having had its proposal defeated it will continue to maintain its reservations on the draft laws and will not enter into any debate concerning them. Some deputies, including Jassim Al Qatami, have retorted that such a refusal to participate in the democratic procedure was hardly consistent with the Government's calls for cooperation between the executive and the legislative bodies. The press have described yesterday's events as a "new crisis in Government/National Assembly relations".

English translation

٣. قالت الحكومة إنها رغم هزيمة اقتراحها ستبقى على تحفظاتها على مشروعات القوانين ولن تدخل في أي نقاش بشأنها. ورد بعض النواب، ومنهم جاسم القطامي، بأن هذا الرفض للمشاركة في الإجراءات الديمقراطية لا يكاد يتفق مع دعوات الحكومة إلى التعاون بين السلطتين التنفيذية والتشريعية. ووصفت الصحافة أحداث الأمس بأنها «أزمة جديدة في العلاقة بين الحكومة ومجلس الأمة».

Notes

The archive's text reads "Al Catami"; the scan reads Al Qatami. The telegram's typeface garbles some letters, corrected here.
