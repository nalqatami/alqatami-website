The rozname of Nakhuda Naser Abdulwahab Al-Qatami: thirteen voyages, 1939 to 1951

What it is

Naser Abdulwahab Al-Qatami’s rozname (sailing log) runs to 244 large pages and records thirteen voyages from Kuwait to India, East Africa and southern Arabia. His first voyage as nakhuda was on the boom «Suhail», owned by Ahmed Mohammed Al-Ghanim, on 22 October 1939; his last was on 20 December 1951, after which he left the sea to work with his father in trade and became a leading merchant, above all in timber from India. He died on 23 March 1995, aged 76. He came from a family of nakhudas: his grandfather Abdulaziz, his father Abdulwahab and his brothers, Nakhuda Bader and Nakhuda Yousef. He trained under his father and his brother Bader on the famous baghla «Al-Badri», first commanded ships of other Kuwaiti merchants and then his father’s, and specialised in seasonal voyages to East Africa, the ports of southern Yemen and the west coast of India, commanding both a baghla and a boom. When the age of the baghla was ending he sold «Al-Badri» in Aden and bought a boom in its place, which he named «Boom Al-Badri» after the baghla he used to call «the sailing school of the sons of Abdulwahab Al-Qatami». With a page of the rozname in his own hand.

Why it matters

A published account of Naser Abdulwahab Al-Qatami’s career at sea, built on his own sailing log, with exact dates for his first and last voyages and the story of Al-Badri.
