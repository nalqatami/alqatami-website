  2. «Notables of Kuwait, no. 6: Eissa bin Abdulwahab Al-Qatami, 1287 to 1347 AH», by Sharqawi. His father died when he was young; he studied the Quran, went to sea with his maternal uncle Thunayan Al-Ghanim, spent years learning navigation and became a captain and guide on the routes to India, Yemen and East Africa. It praises his book Dalil Al-Muhtar and says he died in Muscat in 1347 AH.

  3. «Jassim Al-Qatami and the Contemporary National Movement in Kuwait», by Khaled Saud Al-Zaid, from the third volume of his book on two centuries of Kuwaiti writers. It names him one of three men who carried the cause, with Abdullah Al-Salem and Dr. Ahmad Al-Khatib. It gives his life: born 1927, school in Kuwait, the Cairo police college from 1948, graduating in 1952, law and criminal investigation in London, then Director General of Police from the end of 1953. In 1956 he refused to send the police against the crowds supporting Egypt and resigned; the article prints his resignation letter to Sheikh Sabah Al-Salem in full. He lost his salary and his government house and moved his wife and children into one room of the family home, then ran the Kuwait Cinema Company until his 1 February 1959 Shuwaikh speech calling for a democratic government cost him that job too. After independence he became Undersecretary of Foreign Affairs on 27 March 1962, resigned in January 1963 to stand for the first National Assembly, and was elected again in 1974. It calls the Al-Qatami one of the oldest Kuwaiti families and among the most expert in the sea, naming Captain Eissa bin Abdulwahab Al-Qatami and his Dalil Al-Muhtar, and remembers Jassim as one of the first Kuwaiti short story writers, an early scout and sportsman.
    1 November 1981 · Al-Bayan magazine, Kuwait, issue 188, pages 4 to 11 (Al-Sharekh Archive and Kazima) · Jassim Abdulaziz Al-Qatami

  36. «Sharifa Al-Qatami, the first Kuwaiti woman to work at an oil company.» Born in 1937, daughter of Nakhuda Abdulwahab bin Eissa and Amna bint Abdulaziz Al-Qatami. Her father sent his daughters to the French mission school in Basra and then to Britain, where she studied for two years in London and earned a diploma in management and secretarial work. In 1958 she joined the Kuwait Oil Company at its headquarters in Ahmadi, opening the way for Kuwaiti women to work. She married her relative Khalifa Ahmad Al-Ghanim, and they had four children, Ali, Abdulwahab, Suleiman and Mariam; she later married Shaheen Al-Ghanim. She died on 15 March 2020, aged 83, and was buried at Sulaibikhat.
    9 June 2024 · Al-Bayan, «Personalities under the microscope», by Dr Abdullah Al-Madani · Sharifa Abdulwahab Al-Qatami
